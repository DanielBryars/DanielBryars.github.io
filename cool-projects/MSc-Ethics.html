OCOM5202M / Ethics of AI

Power, Not Just Performance

The assessed work covered discussion posts and a scripted presentation exploring facial recognition, mass surveillance, copyright, generative AI, public policy and the difference between technical capability and social permission.

I have not reproduced third-party images or teaching material here. The useful portfolio point is the thinking: AI ethics is not an afterword to engineering, it is part of deciding what should be built, deployed or resisted.

ETHICS FILENO RAW MATERIAL

This page avoids publishing third-party slide imagery and focuses on the arguments I developed.

surveillance / copyright / policy / accountability

Surveillance

Correct systems can still be wrong.

Facial recognition was treated both as a technical risk when it fails and as a civil-liberties risk when it works exactly as designed.

Generative AI

Data has politics.

The copyright and training-data work pushed beyond “can we train this?” into consent, attribution, economic displacement and control.

Policy

Law lags deployment.

The module made regulation feel less abstract: once systems are embedded, changing social defaults is much harder.

Lesson

Engineering needs a conscience.

The practical lesson was to ask who benefits, who is exposed to risk and what power asymmetries the system amplifies.
